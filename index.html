3. Lawful bases and data protection rights

Under UK data protection law, we must have a “lawful basis” for collecting and using your personal information. There is a list of possible lawful bases in the UK GDPR. You can find out more about lawful bases on the ICO’s website.

3.1 Understanding your rights

Which lawful basis we rely on may affect your data protection rights, which are set out in brief below. You can find out more about your rights and the exemptions which may apply on the ICO’s website.

  • Your right of access – You have the right to ask us for copies of your personal information. You can request other information such as details about where we get personal information from and who we share it with. There are some exemptions, which means you may not receive all the information you ask for.
  • Your right to rectification – You have the right to ask us to correct or delete personal information you think is inaccurate or incomplete.
  • Your right to erasure – You have the right to ask us to delete your personal information.
  • Your right to restriction of processing – You have the right to ask us to limit how we can use your personal information.
  • Your right to object to processing – You have the right to object to the processing of your personal data.
  • Your right to data portability – You have the right to ask that we transfer the personal information you gave us to another organisation, or to you.
  • Your right to withdraw consent – When we use consent as our lawful basis you have the right to withdraw your consent at any time.

If you make a request, we must respond to you without undue delay and in any event within one month. To make a data protection rights request, please contact us using the contact details above.

3.2 Our lawful bases for the collection and use of your data

(a) To comply with legal requirements: Legal obligation – we have to collect or use your information so we can comply with the law. All of your data protection rights may apply, except the right to erasure, the right to object and the right to data portability.

(b) For recruitment purposes: Contract – we have to collect or use the information so we can enter into or carry out a contract with you. All of your data protection rights may apply except the right to object.

(c) For information updates and marketing purposes: Consent – you have provided us with your consent to receive information updates or marketing material.

(d) To select, retain and manage relationships with clients and suppliers: Legitimate interests – we’re collecting or using your information because it benefits you, our organisation or someone else, without causing an undue risk of harm to you. All of your data protection rights may apply, except the right to portability. Our legitimate interests are explained below.

Purpose and legitimacy

We collect and hold business contact details (such as names, corporate email addresses, phone numbers, and job titles) of individuals acting on behalf of corporate entities, partnerships, and legal bodies that are potential, current, or former clients. The processing is legitimate because organisations can only establish relationships, communicate, enter into contracts, and fulfil statutory duties through individual human representatives.

Maintaining accurate records of authorised points of contact is essential for operational administration, contract performance, financial audit requirements, and legal compliance (including fulfilling statutory accounting and record-keeping obligations).

Necessity

Processing this specific personal information is strictly necessary because there is no practical, less intrusive way to conduct business-to-business communications or fulfil our statutory and legal obligations toward corporate entities.

Engaging, servicing, billing, and maintaining auditable transaction trails inherently require communicating with and identifying specific individuals representing the client organisation.

We restrict the data collected solely to professional business contact information required for these explicit purposes.

Balancing test

Benefits to our organisation: enables lawful trading, effective contract execution, business continuity, and adherence to legal and regulatory standards.

Risks and impact on individuals: the data processed is strictly confined to individuals operating in their professional capacity within a commercial setting. It involves non-sensitive, low-risk business contact details rather than private personal data.

Fairness and safeguards: the processing carries minimal risk to the individual’s fundamental rights and freedoms, as professional contact within a commercial relationship aligns with their reasonable expectations. Appropriate technical and organisational security measures are applied to protect the data from unauthorised access. Individuals retain clear rights under applicable data protection laws, including the right to object to processing and request access or rectification, where relevant.

The benefits of maintaining compliant, secure commercial communications and fulfilling statutory obligations substantially outweigh the minimal privacy impact on the individuals involved.

For more information on our use of legitimate interests as a lawful basis you can contact us using the contact details set out above.
